# Scoring method

This document explains, in plain English, every number the app computes and how it is read. Each setting named in `code font` lives in `backend/config/profiles.yaml`.

> Signals are generated by rules from public data. This is not investment advice. Consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser before acting.

Contents: **Part 1** technical readings · **Part 2** fundamental readings · **Part 3** scores, signals, stop-loss and targets, position sizing and tax notes · **Part 4** mutual funds · **Part 5** portfolio analytics · **Part 6** screener · **Part 7** watchlist.

## Part 1: Technical readings

The technical engine reads a stock's chart on each timeframe of your investing style and produces **observations**. Each observation has:

- **Text** with the actual numbers, e.g. *"RSI(14) 28.4: oversold (at or below 30)"*
- A **direction**: bullish ▲, bearish ▼ or neutral ●
- A **category**: trend, momentum, volatility, volume, structure, relative strength or pattern
- A **minor** flag for weak evidence (candlestick patterns, Bollinger stretch)

Phase 6 turns observations into the technical score (0–100). This phase only reads the chart.

### Timeframes per style

| Style | Timeframes analysed (`styles.*.timeframes`) |
|---|---|
| Intraday / short-term | 15-minute, hourly, daily |
| Swing | daily |
| Positional | daily, weekly |
| Long-term | weekly, monthly |
| Custom | daily |

Periods are counted in bars of the timeframe: "SMA 200" is 200 days on a daily chart and 200 weeks on a weekly one. Weekly and monthly bars are built from daily bars. If the current week or month isn't over yet, the report says its bar is still forming.

### When data is missing

An indicator that doesn't have enough bars isn't calculated. It is listed under **Not available** with the reason, e.g. *"SMA 200 (needs 200 bars, have 60)"*, which is common on monthly charts. Missing values are never filled in or estimated. In Phase 6, missing inputs lower the **confidence** of a signal, not its score.

### How the indicators are calculated

The definitions follow the standard textbook formulas as implemented by TA-Lib. The test suite checks the app's values against TA-Lib's output on a 400-bar reference series (`backend/tests/fixtures/technical_reference.csv`).

| Indicator | Definition |
|---|---|
| SMA *n* | Average of the last *n* closes |
| EMA *n* | Exponential average, α = 2/(n+1), seeded with the SMA of the first *n* closes |
| RSI 14 | Wilder's RSI: 100 × avg gain / (avg gain + avg loss), Wilder-smoothed. Flat prices read as 50 |
| MACD 12/26/9 | EMA12 − EMA26; signal = EMA9 of MACD; histogram = MACD − signal. (TA-Lib seeds its first values slightly differently; the two agree after the first ~100 bars.) |
| Stochastic 14/3/3 | Raw %K = position of the close within the 14-bar high–low range; %K = 3-bar average of that; %D = 3-bar average of %K |
| ROC 20 | % change in the close over 20 bars |
| ADX 14, +DI, −DI | Wilder's directional movement system. ADX measures trend *strength*; +DI vs −DI shows its *direction* |
| Supertrend 10, 3 | A trailing line 3 × ATR(10) from the bar's midpoint. It sits below price in an uptrend and flips when a close crosses it |
| Bollinger 20, 2 | 20-bar SMA ± 2 standard deviations (population). %B = where the close sits between the bands (0 = lower, 1 = upper) |
| ATR 14 | Wilder-smoothed true range (includes gaps from the previous close) |
| Historical volatility 20 | Standard deviation of daily log returns × √252, annualised |
| Volume ratio | Today's volume ÷ average of the *previous* 20 bars |
| OBV | On-balance volume: running total of volume, added on up closes and subtracted on down closes. Its trend is the slope over 20 bars ÷ average volume |
| Delivery % | From NSE's daily bhavcopy: share of traded quantity actually delivered (EQ series only) |

### How each reading is interpreted

| Reading | Bullish ▲ when | Bearish ▼ when | Setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price vs 200 / 50 SMA | Close above | Close below | — |
| 50 vs 200 SMA | Golden cross within the last 10 bars, or 50 above 200 | Death cross, or 50 below 200 | `cross_lookback_bars` |
| EMA 9 vs 21 | 9 above 21 (a fresh cross is called out) | 9 below 21 | `cross_lookback_bars` |
| ADX | ADX ≥ 25 with +DI > −DI | ADX ≥ 25 with −DI > +DI | `adx_trending`, `adx_weak` (below 20 = "no clear trend", neutral) |
| Supertrend | Up | Down | — |
| RSI | ≤ 30 (oversold, likely to bounce) or above 55 (positive momentum) | ≥ 70 (overbought) or below 45 (weak momentum) | `rsi_oversold`, `rsi_overbought`, `rsi_neutral_band` (45–55 = neutral) |
| MACD | Above its signal line | Below its signal line | `cross_lookback_bars` for "fresh" crosses |
| Stochastic | %K ≤ 20 | %K ≥ 80 | `stoch_oversold`, `stoch_overbought` |
| ROC | Positive | Negative | — |
| Bollinger (minor) | Close below the lower band | Close above the upper band | A **squeeze** (narrowest band width in 120 bars) is neutral: "a large move often follows" (`squeeze_lookback_bars`) |
| Volume | ≥ 1.5× average on an up move | ≥ 1.5× average on a down move | `volume_surge` |
| OBV | Rising (or rising while price fell: bullish divergence) | Falling (or falling while price rose: bearish divergence) | `obv_slope_min` (smaller slopes = flat) |
| Delivery % | ≥ 1.2× its 20-session average on an up day | Same on a down day | `delivery_surge`, `periods.delivery_avg` |
| 52-week range | Within 5% of the 52-week high | Within 5% of the 52-week low | `near_52w_pct` |
| Breakout / breakdown | Close clears a resistance level by > 1% in the last 3 bars | Close breaks a support level by > 1% | `support_resistance.*`; "on 1.5× volume" or "without a volume surge (less reliable)" is stated |
| Relative strength | Beat Nifty 50 over 3 / 6 months | Lagged Nifty 50 | `periods.relative_strength` |
| Candles (minor) | Bullish engulfing; hammer after a decline | Bearish engulfing | `candle_doji_body`, `candle_hammer_shadow` (doji = neutral) |

ATR, historical volatility, and the nearest support and resistance are shown as neutral information. Phase 6 uses ATR for stop-losses, and support/resistance for entry and target zones.

### Support and resistance

1. Find **swing highs and lows** in the last 250 bars: a bar whose high (or low) is the strict extreme of the 5 bars either side (`pivot_window`). The newest 5 bars can't be confirmed yet.
2. **Cluster** pivots that lie within 1.5% of each other (`cluster_pct`). A cluster touched at least twice (`min_touches`) becomes a level.
3. The nearest level below the close is **support**; the nearest above is **resistance**. If there is none above, the stock is at the top of its range, and the report says so.
4. A **breakout** is a close through a level that already existed before the move, by more than `breakout_buffer_pct`.

### Relative strength

(1 + stock return) ÷ (1 + Nifty 50 return) − 1, over 63 sessions (3 months) and 126 sessions (6 months), using only the dates both series share.

### Data notes

- Prices are delayed end-of-day data from Yahoo Finance, and only completed sessions are used. Intraday bars (15m/1h) are delayed and limited to about 60 days (15m) or 2 years (1h).
- Delivery % covers NSE's EQ series only. BE/BZ stocks are delivery-only by rule, so their 100% says nothing. Indices and BSE-only stocks have no delivery data.
- Indices are shown in points, not rupees, and have no reliable volume, so volume readings are marked unavailable for them.

## Part 2: Fundamental readings

The fundamental engine reads a company's financial statements and produces metrics. Each metric shows its **basis** (which figures and which year it uses), and where possible the **sector median**. It also produces **observations** in the same style as Part 1, marked positive ▲, negative ▼ or neutral ●, in these categories: valuation, profitability, growth, health, quality and ownership. All settings are in the `fundamental:` section.

### Where the numbers come from

| Data | Source |
|---|---|
| Statements: P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, quarters | Yahoo Finance (about 4 years plus about 5 quarters), **or** your Screener.in company export (about 10 years plus 10 quarters) |
| Ownership: promoter holding and change, pledge %, FII/DII holdings and changes | Your Screener.in screen export only |
| Sector | NSE's industry classification (Nifty 500 list); Yahoo's sector for other stocks |
| Price | The latest cached close (Part 1's data), else Yahoo's price |

**One source per stock.** If you've uploaded a Screener.in company export that is at least as recent as Yahoo's data, all statements come from it. Otherwise all come from Yahoo. The two are never mixed line by line, because one may be consolidated and the other standalone, and mixing would distort growth rates. Line items missing *entirely* from the Screener.in export (current assets and liabilities, capex) are taken from Yahoo for the same years, and the report says so.

Screener.in *screen* values (e.g. "Sales growth 5Years") only fill gaps. A value calculated from the statements always wins. Uploads older than `screener_max_age_days` (120) are ignored, and the report tells you.

All amounts are in ₹ crore. Fiscal years are labelled the Indian way: a year ending March 2026 is FY2026.

### How each metric is calculated

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Market cap | Latest close × shares outstanding |
| P/E | Market cap ÷ net profit over the last four quarters (TTM). Falls back to the latest fiscal year if those four quarters aren't consecutive in the data. Not shown for loss-makers |
| P/B | Market cap ÷ shareholders' equity |
| EV/EBITDA | (Market cap + total debt − cash) ÷ EBITDA |
| PEG | P/E ÷ 3-year profit CAGR (only when profit grew) |
| Dividend yield | Dividends paid in the latest year ÷ market cap |
| ROE | Net profit ÷ average equity (this year and last) |
| ROCE | EBIT ÷ (total assets − current liabilities); equity + debt if current liabilities are missing |
| Operating / net margin | Operating profit (or EBIT) ÷ revenue; net profit ÷ revenue. The "change" is percentage points over the last 3 years |
| Revenue / profit CAGR | (latest ÷ value N years earlier)^(1/N) − 1, for N = 3 and 5. Needs N+1 years of results and a positive base year |
| Latest quarter YoY | Latest quarter vs the same quarter a year earlier |
| Debt/equity | Total debt ÷ equity |
| Interest coverage | EBIT ÷ interest expense |
| Current ratio | Current assets ÷ current liabilities |
| Free cash flow | Cash from operations − capital expenditure; how many of the last 3 years it was positive |

### Banks, NBFCs and insurers

For companies in `financial_sectors`, borrowing *is* the business. Debt/equity, ROCE, EV/EBITDA, operating margin, current ratio, free cash flow and the Piotroski F-score would all mislead there, so they are skipped and the report says why. P/E, P/B, ROE, net margin, growth and ownership still apply.

### Data cross-check

Yahoo publishes its own P/E, P/B and EV/EBITDA, and the app calculates the same ratios from Yahoo's statements. If the two differ by more than `cross_check_tolerance` (25%), the report shows a "Data check" note with both figures. A Screener.in export settles it. The app doesn't silently pick one.

### Sector medians

Medians are taken over **cached peers**: other stocks in the same NSE industry whose fundamentals have been fetched. You need at least `sector_min_peers` (5) peers, otherwise the report says "no sector median" and gives the count. **Refresh fundamentals + peers** (Settings → Data refresh, or automatically every Saturday 10:00 IST) fetches up to `sector_max_peers` (40) Nifty 500 peers for each industry you hold. Medians cover P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, ROE, ROCE, operating margin, debt/equity and 3-year revenue growth.

### Piotroski F-score

The F-score has nine pass/fail tests on the last two fiscal years, one point each. The report shows every test and the numbers behind it:

1. **Profitable:** ROA (net profit ÷ opening assets) > 0
2. **Cash from operations** > 0
3. **ROA improving** vs last year
4. **Earnings quality:** cash from operations > net profit
5. **Leverage not rising:** long-term debt ÷ assets ≤ last year
6. **Current ratio improving**
7. **No new shares issued** (dilution)
8. **Gross margin improving**
9. **Asset turnover improving:** revenue ÷ opening assets

A score is given **only when all 9 tests can be computed**. Otherwise the report shows how many could be. Screener.in's export doesn't separate long-term debt, so its total borrowings stand in for test 5.

### How each reading is interpreted

| Reading | Positive ▲ | Negative ▼ | Settings |
|---|---|---|---|
| P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA vs sector | ≤ 0.8 × median | ≥ 1.2 × median | `valuation_cheap_ratio`, `valuation_rich_ratio` |
| PEG | ≤ 1 | ≥ 2 | `peg_cheap`, `peg_rich` |
| Dividend yield (minor) | ≥ 3% | — | `dividend_yield_high` |
| ROE, ROCE | ≥ 15% | ≤ 8% | `roe_good/poor`, `roce_good/poor` |
| Margin change (3y) | up > 1 pp | down > 1 pp | `margin_trend_flat_pp` |
| Revenue/profit CAGR | ≥ 15% a year | < 0 | `growth_good`, `growth_poor` |
| Latest quarter YoY | ≥ +10% | < 0 | `quarter_yoy_good/poor` |
| Debt/equity | ≤ 0.3 | **above your risk level's `max_debt_to_equity`** (e.g. 1.0 for Medium) | `debt_equity_low`, `risk_levels.*` |
| Interest coverage | ≥ 5× | < 2× | `interest_coverage_good/poor` |
| Current ratio | ≥ 1.5 | < 1.0 | `current_ratio_good/poor` |
| Free cash flow | positive in all of the last 3 years | negative now, and positive in at most 1 of 3 years | — |
| Losses | — | net loss in the latest year(s), flagged "while debt rose" if it did | — |
| F-score | ≥ 7 | ≤ 3 | `fscore_strong`, `fscore_weak` |
| Promoter stake change | +1 pp or more | −1 pp or more | `promoter_change_pp` |
| Pledge | 0% (minor) | > 10%; > 25% is a **severe red flag** | `pledge_warn_pct`, `signals.severe_flags.max_pledge_pct` |
| FII/DII stake change | +1 pp or more | −1 pp or more | `institution_change_pp` |

The severe flags (pledge above 25%, or losses with rising debt) force a Sell signal; see Part 3.

## Part 3: Scores and signals

Every reading from Parts 1 and 2 feeds a score. Nothing is hidden: the API and the **Signal** tab show each reading with the exact number of points it adds to or takes from the composite.

### Sub-scores (0–100)

Readings are grouped by category. Each category's sub-score is

> **sub-score = 50 + 50 × Σ(direction × weight) / Σ(weight)**

where direction is +1 for a bullish reading, −1 for bearish and 0 for neutral. So all-bullish gives 100, all-bearish 0, and an even or neutral picture 50.

Reading weights (`scoring:`):
- Every reading starts at **1.0**.
- Stronger evidence counts more (`reading_weights`): a golden or death cross 2.0, a strong ADX trend 1.5, a breakout or breakdown 1.5, debt above your limit 2.0, losses 2.0, an F-score of 7+ or 3− 1.5, a high pledge 1.5 (a severe one 3.0), promoter selling 1.5.
- Minor evidence (candles, a Bollinger stretch, a high dividend, no pledge) counts × `minor_weight` (0.5).
- A neutral reading counts × `neutral_weight` (0.5). It pulls its sub-score toward 50 without deciding it.
- Readings that describe rather than judge are shown but not scored (`info_only`): the ATR size, historical volatility, the support/resistance map, and P/E or P/B when there is no sector median to compare against.

### Technical score

| Category | Weight | Readings |
|---|---|---|
| Trend | 30% | price vs 50/200 SMA, crosses, EMA 9/21, ADX, Supertrend |
| Momentum | 20% | RSI, MACD, Stochastic, ROC |
| Relative strength | 20% | 3- and 6-month performance vs Nifty 50 |
| Volume | 15% | volume surges, OBV trend/divergence, delivery % |
| Structure | 15% | breakouts/breakdowns, 52-week high/low zone |
| Volatility | 0% | not a direction: volatility sets the stop-loss distance instead |
| Patterns | 0% | candlesticks are shown as reasons only |

Category weights are re-normalised over the categories that have data. A style with several timeframes scores each one separately (with the daily-only context readings such as relative strength and delivery added to each) and blends them:
- the style's first timeframe gets `primary_timeframe_weight` (60%);
- the others share the rest.

For example, Positional is daily 60% + weekly 40%, and Intraday is 15-min 60% + hourly 20% + daily 20%.

### Fundamental score

| Category | Weight | Readings |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation | 20% | P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA vs sector median; PEG; dividend yield |
| Profitability | 20% | ROE, ROCE, operating/net margin trend |
| Growth | 20% | revenue/profit CAGR (3y, 5y), latest quarter YoY |
| Financial health | 20% | debt/equity vs your limit, interest cover, current ratio, free cash flow, losses |
| Quality | 10% | Piotroski F-score |
| Ownership | 10% | promoter stake and pledge, FII/DII changes (Screener.in upload) |

### Composite score and confidence

**Composite = technical weight × technical score + fundamental weight × fundamental score.** The weights come from your style (Intraday 90/10, Swing 70/30, Positional 50/50, Long-term 20/80, Custom = your slider).

- If one side has no score, the composite is the other side alone, and a note says so.
- ETFs and indices have no fundamentals, so their composite is technical only. This doesn't count as missing data.
- **Missing data lowers confidence, not the score.** *Coverage* is the weighted share of categories that had data:
  - technical: categories × timeframes;
  - fundamental: categories, with health and quality not expected for banks and insurers (`financial_exempt`).
  - The overall coverage is style-weighted.
- **Confidence** is **High** at 80%+ coverage (`high_min_coverage`), **Medium** at 50%+, and **Low** below that. Stale prices or fundamentals cap it at Medium (`stale_caps_at`).

Each reading's **points** are its exact share of the composite. Σ points = composite − 50, so you can audit the score line by line. The top 3 bullish and bearish reasons are the readings with the most points either way.

### Red flags (risk overlays from your risk level)

| Flag | When | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Stop-loss breached | the latest completed bar closed below the ATR trailing stop (below) | **severe** for a holding, if the style's `stop_breach_exits` is true; otherwise a red flag |
| Pledge | promoter pledge above `max_pledge_pct` (25%) | **severe** |
| Pledge | above `pledge_warn_pct` (10%) | red flag |
| Losses with rising debt | net loss for `consecutive_loss_years` (2) years in a row while debt rose | **severe** |
| High debt | debt/equity above your `max_debt_to_equity` | red flag |
| High beta | beta vs Nifty 50 above your `max_beta` (from a year of daily returns; `beta_lookback_sessions`) | red flag |
| Sector concentration | the stock's sector is above your `max_weight_per_sector` | red flag |

The **Long-term** style has `stop_breach_exits: false`. Long-term investors usually ride out pullbacks, so a breached stop is a warning that blocks buying but doesn't force a sale. Set it to `true` if you want long-term holdings to exit on stops too.

### Signal rules (checked in this order)

| Signal | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Sell / Exit** | any severe flag, **or** composite < `reduce_floor` (35) |
| **Reduce / Trim** | position above your `max_weight_per_stock`, **or** composite 35 to < 45 (`hold_floor`) |
| **Strong Buy / Add** | composite ≥ buy threshold + `strong_buy_margin` (10), no red flags, position below the max weight |
| **Buy / Add** | composite ≥ buy threshold (Low 75 / **Medium 70** / High 65), no red flags |
| **Hold** | everything else, including a buy-level score held back by a red flag |
| **Insufficient data** | no composite and none of the Sell/Reduce triggers. The app never guesses. |

For a stock you don't hold, Reduce or Sell reads as "avoid".

### Stop-loss and targets

These are computed on the style's main timeframe, using completed bars only. If that timeframe has too little history, daily bars are used.

- **ATR trailing stop** = highest high of the last `stop_loss_lookback_bars` (22) bars − your risk level's ATR multiple (Low 1.5×, **Medium 2×**, High 3×) × ATR(14). A close below it is the "stop breached" flag.
- **Stop for a new purchase** = close − multiple × ATR. That distance is **R**, the risk per share.
- **Targets**: the nearest resistance above the price, plus close + 2R and close + 3R (`target_r_multiples`).

### Position size suggestions

- **Weights** are shares of your whole portfolio (stocks + mutual funds; `rebalance.weight_base`). Values use the latest cached close, else the price in your import.
- **Buy** aims for `buy_target` × your max weight (75% of 12% = 9% at Medium); **Strong Buy** aims for the full max weight (`strong_buy_target`).
- The quantity to add assumes new money: it solves (value + x) / (total + x) = target. It is capped so the sector stays within its limit, and rounded down to whole shares.
- **Reduce for being overweight**: trims back to the max weight, solving (value − x) / (total − x) = max weight, rounded up.
- **Reduce on a weak score**: trims to `reduce_keep` (50%) of the position.
- **Sell**: the whole position.
- Trades smaller than `min_trade_value_inr` (₹1,000) are not suggested. These are suggestions to review; the app never places orders.

### Tax note

- **Holding period**: equity held more than `equity_ltcg_min_holding_days` (365) days is long-term.
- **Which shares are sold**: first in, first out across your lots, so the oldest shares go first.
- **Estimate**: the note shows the gain or loss on the suggested sale and an indicative tax at `equity_stcg_rate` / `equity_ltcg_rate`.
  - The yearly LTCG exemption (`equity_ltcg_exemption_inr`) is mentioned but not applied, because it is shared across all your sales.
  - The estimate excludes cess.
- **Missing dates**: Groww's holdings export has no purchase date, so the note says the term is unknown rather than guessing.
- **Rates change with each Budget: verify them before acting.**

### Signal history

Each time holdings' signals are computed, whether on the Holdings page or by the daily refresh, one row per stock, day, style and risk level is saved. The Signal tab shows that history.

## Part 4: Mutual funds

Settings are in `mf:`.

### Returns
- **Scheme CAGR (1, 3, 5 years)**: point to point, from the NAV on or before the start date to the latest NAV. A period the NAV history doesn't cover (within 7 days) is shown as "—".
- **Your XIRR**: the annualised return on your own cash flows.
  - **With a detailed CAS**: every purchase, SIP and stamp duty is money in; every redemption, switch-out and dividend payout is money out. Reinvested dividends aren't cash flows. The current value at today's NAV is the final inflow.
  - **Approximate**: without transactions, if the first purchase date and amount invested are known, the XIRR assumes one lump sum on that date and is labelled approximate. Otherwise it is not shown.
- **Rolling returns**: the fund's and the benchmark's CAGR over every `rolling_windows_years` window (1 and 3 years), with a window starting every `rolling_step_days` (7) days over their common history. The summary shows:
  - the average of each, and the average difference (pp a year);
  - the share of windows the fund beat the index;
  - the worst and best difference.

### Risk (over the last `risk_stats_years` = 3 years, or all history if shorter; at least `min_history_days`)
- **Volatility**: standard deviation of NAV-to-NAV returns, annualised by the number of NAVs a year.
- **Sharpe**: (CAGR − risk-free rate) / volatility.
- **Sortino**: the same, using only downside deviation (returns below the risk-free rate). The rate is `risk_free_rate_pct` (6.5%, roughly the 91-day T-bill): **verify and update it**.
- **Max drawdown**: the worst fall from a previous peak, with its dates.

The benchmark's figures cover the same period.

### Benchmark
Each fund is compared with the first `benchmarks` entry whose keywords appear in its AMFI category (or its name, for index funds and ETFs). Debt and "other" funds (international, gold, FoFs) get none (`asset_classes`).

Yahoo only has **price** indices, without dividends, so funds look about 1–1.5% a year better than against the official total-return index. Keep this in mind for index funds: tracking the index looks like beating it.

### Cost
- **Regular vs Direct**: the plan comes from the AMFI scheme name. For a Regular plan the app finds the Direct plan of the same scheme (same AMC, option and name) and measures the cost gap as Direct CAGR − Regular CAGR over 3 years (else 1 year).
- **Expense ratio (TER)**: no free API publishes it, so you can enter it on the fund page. A TER above `expense_ratio_high_pct` (equity 1.0%, debt 0.5%) triggers Review.

### Overlap
From the portfolio holdings you upload, overlap between two funds is **Σ min(weight in A, weight in B)** over the stocks they share, matched by ISIN (else by name). It is marked **approximate** when either file covers less than `overlap_min_coverage_pct` (90%) of the fund, is older than `portfolio_max_age_days` (100), or has no date.

Funds against the same benchmark count as the same segment: more than `duplicate_category_max` (2), e.g. three large-cap funds, is a warning.

### Signal (`mf.signals`; Switch beats Review beats Continue)
| Signal | When |
|---|---|
| **Switch** | a Regular plan with a Direct twin (`regular_plan_switch`), **or** rolling 3-year returns average ≤ `switch_rolling_excess_pp` (−3 pp) behind the benchmark **and** beat it in ≤ `switch_max_beat_share` (30%) of windows |
| **Review** | rolling 3-year returns average < `review_rolling_excess_pp` (−1 pp) behind; last year > `review_1y_excess_pp` (5 pp) behind; TER above the limit; overlap with another of your funds > `overlap_high_pct` (50%); more funds in the same segment than `duplicate_category_max` |
| **Continue** | none of the above (said so when there is little history) |
| **Insufficient data** | no NAV history yet |

Every rule that fires is listed, along with the supporting facts: Sharpe vs benchmark, worst fall vs benchmark, Direct plan.

Switching a fund is a redemption, so check the exit load and capital gains tax first.

## Part 5: Portfolio analytics

Settings are in `portfolio:`.

- **Value**:
  - stocks use the latest cached close × quantity (else the price in your import);
  - funds use the latest NAV × units (else the value in your import).
- **Invested and unrealised P&L**: shown only if the cost of **every** holding is known; otherwise it says which are missing, so P&L is never understated.
- **Day change**: the latest vs previous close or NAV. Holdings without a previous value are listed as left out.
- **XIRR**: stocks with purchase dates (each lot is money in at quantity × average price on its date) plus funds' cash flows as in Part 4. The current value of those holdings is the final inflow.
  - **Coverage** is the share of the portfolio value included.
  - Below `xirr_min_coverage` (50%) it is labelled partial.
  - Groww's holdings export has no dates, so stocks need manual entry with a date.
- **Allocation**: by holding, by sector, by market cap and by asset class.
  - **Sector**: NSE industry; ETFs are "Index ETFs"; funds are one "not split by sector" slice.
  - **Market cap**: stocks are bucketed by Yahoo market cap: large ≥ `large_cap_min_cr` (₹1,00,000 Cr), mid ≥ `mid_cap_min_cr` (₹33,000 Cr). These approximate SEBI's half-yearly AMFI ranking (top 100 / 101–250): **verify**. Funds take their category's segment.
  - **Asset class**: stocks & ETFs vs equity, debt, hybrid and other funds.
- **Concentration warnings**: any stock above your max weight per stock, any sector above your max weight per sector, and more funds in one segment than allowed. The share held by your top `top_n_concentration` (5) holdings is also noted.
- **Portfolio beta**: value-weighted beta vs Nifty 50 of each holding's daily returns (prices or NAVs) over up to `scoring.beta_lookback_sessions`. Holdings with less history are left out, and the coverage is shown.
- **Correlation**: pairwise correlation of daily returns for the top `correlation_top_n` (10) holdings over `correlation_lookback_sessions` (252). It is blank where two series share fewer than 60 days.
- **Performance vs Nifty 50**: your **current** holdings valued daily since your first import, both indexed to 100.
  - If the first import is under `performance_min_days` (30) old, the last `performance_fallback_days` (365) days are shown instead.
  - Either way it is labelled as not your actual trading history, because buys and sells in between aren't known.

## Part 6: Screener

Settings are in `screener:`; presets are listed there in full.

### The scan
1. **Universe**: a Nifty index list from NSE, or up to `custom_universe_max` NSE symbols you type (ETFs and unknown symbols are skipped and listed).
2. **Data**, per stock: daily prices (downloaded if missing or stale, unless you chose cached only), then:
   - fewer than `min_history_sessions` (60) bars: **not scored** (recent listing);
   - average daily traded value (close × volume over `traded_value_sessions`, 20) below `min_avg_daily_traded_value_cr` (₹10 Cr): **not scored** (illiquid), and no fundamentals are downloaded for it;
   - otherwise fundamentals are downloaded if missing or stale, and their metrics are stored so sector medians include the whole universe.
   After each stock that needed a download the scan pauses `request_delay_seconds` (0.5 s), on top of the per-source rate limits.
3. **Score**, once all data is in: the same engine as your holdings (Parts 1–3) with your style and risk, from cached data. One stock failing never stops the scan; it is listed with the error.

**Stop** ends the scan after the current stock; the stocks finished so far are kept as a partial scan.

### Filtering a scan (instant)
For each scored stock, in this order:
1. **Already held**: left out unless you include holdings.
2. **Risk pre-filters** (your risk level): market cap ≥ `min_market_cap_cr` (must be known), debt/equity ≤ `max_debt_to_equity` (when known; not for financials), beta ≤ `max_beta` (when known).
3. **Signal**: anything in `exclude_signals` (Sell, Reduce) is never an idea: the engine says avoid it.
4. **The screen**: every condition must hold. Technical fields use **daily** bars whatever your style, so a screen means the same thing under every style.
   - A stock with no value for a field **fails** that condition and is counted as "missing data"; the app never assumes.
   - Fields that don't apply to lenders and insurers (debt/equity, ROCE, EV/EBITDA, operating margin, interest cover, current ratio, FCF years) are **passed** for financials.
   - "P/E vs sector median" is P/E ÷ the sector median P/E (needs `fundamental.sector_min_peers` peers).
5. **Ranking**: composite score, then confidence, then market cap; the top `top_n` (20). Stocks that failed exactly one condition are shown as near misses.

### Presets
| Preset | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Top scores | None: best composite scores |
| Quality at fair price | ROE ≥ 15%, ROCE ≥ 15%, debt/equity ≤ 0.5, P/E ≤ sector median |
| Momentum breakout | Price above the 50-DMA and 200-DMA, within 5% of the 52-week high, volume ≥ 1.5× its 20-day average, ADX > 25 |
| Pullback in uptrend | Price above the 200-DMA, RSI 35–45, within ±3% of the 50-DMA |
| Growth | Revenue and profit CAGR (3y) > 15%, operating margin up over 3 years |
| Dividend + value | Dividend yield ≥ 3%, P/E ≤ sector median, positive free cash flow in ≥ 2 of the last 3 years |

### Entry zone, stop and targets (for a new purchase)
On your style's main timeframe, as in Part 3:
- **Entry zone**: from close − `entry_zone_atr` (0.5) × ATR, or the nearest support if that is higher (and below the close), up to the last close.
- **Stop**: close − your risk level's ATR multiple × ATR.
- **Targets**: the nearest resistance above the close, then 2R and 3R, where R = the stop distance.

### Sector effect
A starter position is the Buy target weight (`rebalance.buy_target` × max weight per stock), bought with new money. The sector's share of your portfolio is shown before and after; it is flagged when it would pass your sector limit.

## Part 7: Watchlist
Each stock is re-scored from cached data when you open the page, with your current style and risk. The first score after adding it is its baseline. The page shows the signal, score and price change since then, and the last date the recorded signal changed. The daily refresh downloads watchlist stocks with your holdings and records their signals in the same signal history.

## Part 8: Backtest helper (Analysis → Backtest)

A **historical test** of the rules on one stock. It shows how the rules behaved in the past and does not predict the future. Settings are under `backtest:` in `profiles.yaml`.

### How the replay works
1. **Days tested.** Every `step_sessions` (5) sessions over the last `years` (3), ending on the latest session. The first evaluation needs `warmup_sessions` (260) of history before it, enough for the 200-DMA and the 52-week range.
2. **No look-ahead.** On each of those days the price history is cut at that day, so nothing after it is visible. A test checks that changing later prices never changes an earlier signal.
3. **Same scoring as the live signal.** The technical engine and technical score run on your style's daily-or-longer timeframes. Intraday bars aren't replayed: Yahoo keeps only weeks of them.
4. **Same signal rules** (`decide`). The ATR trailing stop is treated as the live app treats it: a breach is a Sell when the replay is "in" and your style exits on stops, and otherwise a red flag that blocks a Buy. There is no position weight, so the over-weight rule doesn't apply.

**Left out**, because free sources have no point-in-time history (today's P/E replayed into 2023 would be look-ahead):
- fundamentals;
- delivery %.

So the replayed signal is a technical-only signal. It can differ from today's live signal, which also uses fundamentals and your position.

### What is reported
- **Hit rate.** A Buy or Strong Buy was "right" if the price was higher `horizon_sessions` later. The horizon is per style: swing 20, positional 60, long-term 120. A Reduce or Sell was right if the price was lower. Evaluations too recent to have a full window aren't counted.
- **Base rate.** How often the price was higher after *any* evaluation day. A rule helps only if its hit rate beats this.
- **Average move after** each signal.
- **Following the rules** vs **buy and hold**:
  - The strategy is in after a Buy, out after a Reduce or Sell, and unchanged on a Hold.
  - A change decided at a day's close is traded at the next session's open.
  - Prices are adjusted for splits and bonuses (`adj_close`).
  - For each, the app reports total return, maximum drawdown (the largest fall from a peak) and time in the market.
  - Brokerage, taxes, slippage and dividends are not modelled.

One stock over three years is a small sample, so read the numbers as a sanity check of the rules, not as evidence that they work.

## Part 9: Score Shape (checks)

The Score Shape is a plain-English view of the same data. Each stock gets **six axes**. Each axis scores 0–6, built from simple pass/fail checks. It sits beside the composite score and signal and doesn't change either. The checks are defined in `config/checks.yaml`: labels, thresholds and wording. Where `profiles.yaml` already has a threshold, the check refers to it (`ref:`) instead of copying it, so editing `profiles.yaml` (or a threshold in Settings) moves both.

### Axes and checks (stocks)

| Axis | Checks |
|---|---|
| **Valuation** | P/E below the sector median · P/E below its own median at recent year-ends · PEG at most 1.5 · price below the estimated fair value · EV/EBITDA below the sector median (not for lenders) · P/B within `valuation_rich_ratio` × the sector median |
| **Future Growth** | Revenue CAGR (3y) ≥ `growth_good` · profit CAGR (3y) ≥ `growth_good` · latest quarter revenue YoY ≥ `quarter_yoy_good` · same for profit · latest quarter's revenue growth faster than the 3-year average · profit growing faster than revenue |
| **Past Performance** | ROE ≥ `roe_good` · ROE above the sector median · ROCE ≥ `roce_good` (not for lenders) · operating margin above the sector median (not for lenders) · margin change over 3 years no worse than −`margin_trend_flat_pp` · a profit in every year on record |
| **Financial Health** | Debt/equity within your risk level's `max_debt_to_equity` · interest coverage ≥ 3× (no interest to pay passes) · current ratio ≥ `current_ratio_poor` · positive free cash flow · debt/equity lower now than in the oldest year on record (just debt when equity is missing) · promoter pledge under 5% (needs a Screener.in upload) |
| **Momentum** | Price above the 200-day average · price above the 50-day average · RSI between `rsi_neutral_band[0]` and `rsi_overbought` · MACD above its signal line · beating the Nifty 50 over 6 months · on-balance volume rising faster than `obv_slope_min` |
| **Dividends** | Pays a dividend · yield ≥ `dividend_yield_high` · paid in every year on record · dividends paid higher than in the oldest year · payout at most 75% of profit · free cash flow covers the dividend (not for lenders) |

Notes on the axes:

- **Future Growth** uses recent results only. The app has no analyst forecasts.
- **Momentum** always uses daily bars, whatever your style, so a stock's shape is the same under every style. Style changes only the order and emphasis on screen.
- **Lenders and insurers.** The checks marked "not for lenders" are left out. Financial Health uses lender checks instead:
  - equity at least 8% of total assets;
  - book value (equity) higher than in the oldest year;
  - return on assets ≥ 1%;
  - the pledge check.
- **Non-payers.** A company that pays no dividend fails every dividend check, with the reason "Pays no dividend". Its Dividends axis shows 0 with a "No dividend" note.
- **Statements in another currency** (some IT companies file in USD). Valuation checks, P/E history and the fair value are skipped, as the fundamental engine already does for those ratios. Amounts are labelled in that currency.

### Axis score, total and confidence

- **Check result.** Each check is **pass**, **fail** or **no data**. Every result shows the value, the threshold and one sentence.
- **Axis score.** `passed ÷ checks with data × 6`, with halves rounded up. With all 6 checks computable, this is simply the number passed.
  - Checks with no data, and checks that don't apply, are left out.
  - With fewer than `axis.min_applicable` checks that have data (3 for stocks), the axis shows **Not enough data** instead of a score.
- **Total.** The sum of the axes that have a score, out of 6 × those axes (36 when all six have one).
- **Colour step** (1 = weak … 5 = strong). Set by total ÷ maximum against `axis.score_steps` (0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8). The number is always shown next to the colour.
- **Confidence.** The share of checks with data, against the cut-offs in `confidence`: high ≥ 0.8, medium ≥ 0.5, low otherwise.

### Rewards and Risks, summary

- **Rewards.** Passed checks marked `highlight: [reward]` in the YAML.
- **Risks.** The stock's red flags (severe first), then failed checks marked `highlight: [risk]`.
- Each list keeps 5 items at most.
- **Summary.** Filled in from a template, not written by an AI: checks passed, strongest and weakest axis, missing data, the fair-value gap and the signal under your style.

### Fair value estimate

Fair value is labelled **Estimate**. It is the average of the methods that have a sector median (needs `fundamental.sector_min_peers` cached peers):

- **Earnings:** sector-median P/E × trailing profit, which works out as market cap × median P/E ÷ the company's P/E.
- **Cash profit** (non-financials): sector-median EV/EBITDA × EBITDA, minus debt, plus cash. EBITDA, debt and cash are the same figures used for the EV/EBITDA metric.
- **Book value** (lenders and insurers): sector-median P/B × equity.

Each is turned into a per-share value at today's share count. A negative result (debt worth more than the business at peer multiples) counts as zero.

What it means and doesn't:

- It says what the company would be worth if the market priced it like its peers. It doesn't allow for faster growth or better returns, which can justify a premium.
- A single method (P/E alone) would duplicate the "P/E below the sector median" check, so two methods are averaged.

### P/E history

- **Each year-end's P/E.** The close on (or up to a week before) the financial year-end × shares ÷ that year's net profit. It uses the year's share count when the statements have it, and today's otherwise.
- **Loss years** are skipped.
- **The check** needs at least 3 year-ends. Yahoo usually provides 4, so this is the 3–4 year median, not 5 years.

### Funds

Five axes, scored the same way. An axis needs `axis.min_applicable.fund` = 2 checks with data.

| Axis | Checks |
|---|---|
| **Returns** | Beat the benchmark over 1, 3 and 5 years · 3-year CAGR above `mf.risk_free_rate_pct` |
| **Consistency** | Beat the benchmark in ≥ 50% of rolling 3-year windows · average rolling 3-year excess ≥ `mf.signals.review_rolling_excess_pp` · beat it in ≥ 50% of rolling 1-year windows |
| **Risk** | Volatility no higher than the benchmark's · maximum drawdown no deeper · no overlap above `mf.overlap_high_pct` with your other funds (needs portfolio files) |
| **Risk-adjusted** | Sharpe ratio ≥ the benchmark's · Sortino ratio ≥ the benchmark's |
| **Cost** | Direct plan · expense ratio within `mf.expense_ratio_high_pct` for its asset class (needs your input) |

Debt and "other" funds have no equity benchmark, so most Returns, Consistency and Risk checks show no data for them.

### Portfolio shape

- **Each axis** is the value-weighted average of your stock holdings' scores on that axis. Holdings with no score on an axis are left out of that axis; `coverage` says how much of your stock value it covers.
- **Funds** have different axes, so they aren't averaged in.
- **Portfolio Rewards and Risks** cover:
  - sectors above your sector limit;
  - holdings above your per-stock limit;
  - Sell/Reduce signals;
  - the share of stock value in companies with weak (≤ 2/6) or strong (≥ 5/6) Financial Health.

### History ("changed since last week")

- **What is saved.** Each time holdings' signals are saved (the daily job, or opening Holdings), the stock's axis scores are saved too, one row per stock, day and risk level (`AxisRecord`). Watchlist stocks are saved by the daily job.
- **What "a week ago" means.** The latest saved shape at least 6 days old. The change per axis compares today's scores with it, and the signal on that day comes from the signal history.
- **When it starts.** History begins when this feature is first used, so changes appear after about a week.
